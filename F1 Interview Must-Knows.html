F1 Interview Must-Knows

High-level cheat sheet: physics, engineering, strategy & mindset.

1. How an F1 Team Works (Context)

Core departments
  • Aero Wind tunnel, CFD, aero maps, correlation.
  • Design CAD (e.g. CATIA/NX/SolidWorks), packaging, integration with aero & PU.
  • Vehicle Dynamics Suspension geometry, ride, handling, tire models, lap simulations.
  • Power Unit ICE, turbo, MGU-H (legacy), MGU-K, battery, deployment maps.
  • Strategy Race sims, tire life models, probability & decision making under uncertainty.
  • Electronics & Controls Sensors, ECU, control algorithms, reliability.
  • Manufacturing & Ops Composites, machining, QA, logistics, pit crew.

Key interview idea: always connect your role to lap time and race result.

2. Lap Time, Grip & Vehicle Dynamics

2.1 Cornering & Lateral Acceleration

F1 performance is dominated by cornering. Basic lateral acceleration:

\( a_y = \dfrac{v^2}{R} \)
  • \(a_y\) – lateral acceleration
  • \(v\) – cornering speed
  • \(R\) – corner radius

Maximum lateral acceleration is limited by tire grip:

\( F_y \leq \mu \, N \)
  • \(F_y\) – lateral force
  • \(\mu\) – tire friction coefficient (varies with load, temp, slip)
  • \(N\) – normal load (weight + downforce)
2.2 Lap Time Sensitivities

Teams talk in “sensitivity” terms: \(\Delta t_{\text{lap}}\) per change in parameter.

  • Downforce: huge gains in medium–high speed corners.
  • Drag: hurts top speed, especially at long straights.
  • Weight: every extra kg costs lap time, esp. in traction and braking.
  • Ride height: affects ground-effect downforce and porpoising.

You don’t need exact numbers, but do show qualitative reasoning: trade-offs between these.

2.3 Braking & Longitudinal Dynamics
  • Braking distance (constant decel assumption):
\( s = \dfrac{v^2}{2a} \)
  • Where \(a\) is limited by tire grip and brake system capabilities.
  • ABS is banned → driver & brake balance crucial.

3. Tires – The Core of Performance

Everything (aero, setup, driving style) aims to put tires in the right temperature, load, and slip range.

3.1 Basic Tire Model (Simplified)

Lateral force approx:

\( F_y \approx \mu(N, T) \; N \)
  • \(N\) – normal load (affected by aero & weight transfer).
  • \(T\) – tire temperature (in a narrow “window”).
  • \(\mu\) – not constant; peaks then drops with slip/temperature.
3.2 Practical Interview Points
  • Warm-up: getting tires up to temperature by load (cornering), slip & sometimes weaving.
  • Degradation: loss of grip over laps (thermal, wear, graining, blistering).
  • Pressure: affects contact patch, stiffness, and temperature behavior.
  • Balance: understeer/oversteer often explained by front vs. rear tire state.

4. Aerodynamics – Downforce & Drag

4.1 Core Equations

Downforce and drag are usually written as:

\( F_D = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\rho\,C_D\,A\,v^2 \)
\( F_L = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\rho\,C_L\,A\,v^2 \)
  • \(\rho\) – air density
  • \(C_D\) – drag coefficient
  • \(C_L\) – lift coefficient (negative for downforce)
  • \(A\) – reference area
  • \(v\) – car speed

F1: we usually talk about “downforce” instead of “lift” but use the same math with sign convention.

4.2 Ground Effect & Ride Height
  • Underfloor & diffuser generate a large fraction of downforce.
  • Highly sensitive to ride height and pitch.
  • Too low → flow separation, porpoising, legality plank wear.
4.3 Trade-offs & Interview Style Questions
  • “If you gain 10% more downforce but 1% more drag, how does this affect lap time?”
  • Reason qualitatively:
    • Short tracks with many corners → downforce more valuable.
    • Low-downforce tracks (Monza-type) → drag penalty more painful.
  • Know basics of:
    • CFD (meshing, turbulence models like \(k\text{-}\omega\) SST).
    • Wind-tunnel scaling & correlation with track data.

5. Power Unit & Energy Management (High Level)

5.1 Power & Tractive Force

Relation between engine power \(P\) and tractive force \(F_t\):

\( P = F_t \, v \quad \Rightarrow \quad F_t = \dfrac{P}{v} \)
  • At low speeds: limited by tire grip (traction).
  • At high speeds: limited by power and drag.
5.2 Hybrid Energy (MGU-K & Battery)
  • Harvest energy under braking.
  • Deploy strategically for overtakes, defending, and out-laps/in-laps.
  • Constraints on max power and energy per lap.

Interviewers want: understanding of trade-offs (e.g., save energy early in stint vs. attack now).

6. Data Analysis & Telemetry

6.1 Key Signals
  • Speed vs. distance / time.
  • Throttle position, brake pressure.
  • Steering angle.
  • Gear, engine RPM.
  • Tire temperatures & pressures, sometimes carcass vs. surface.
6.2 Typical Questions
  • “Where is the driver losing time?” → Compare speed traces, braking points, minimum corner speeds.
  • “Is the problem driver or car?” → Look at consistency, balance shifts, correlation with setup changes.
6.3 Tools
  • Languages: Python, MATLAB, sometimes C++ for tools, Excel for quick checks.
  • Show ability to clean data, plot, and draw engineering conclusions.

7. Race Strategy Basics

7.1 Key Concepts
  • Undercut: pit earlier, use fresher tires to gain time vs. rival on old tires.
  • Overcut: stay out longer, exploit clean air or better tire phase.
  • Undercut window: distance/time where rival is vulnerable to an undercut.
  • VSC/SC windows: cheap pit stops — reduced time loss due to slower field.
  • Tire offset: difference in age/compound between cars; matters for overtaking chances.
7.2 Very Simple Strategy Model Idea

Lap time model (conceptual):

\( t_{\text{lap}} = t_0 + \Delta t_{\text{fuel}} + \Delta t_{\text{tires}} + \Delta t_{\text{traffic}} + \dots \)
  • Each stint: lap time drifts due to fuel burn, tire degradation, etc.
  • Pit stop: lose \(\approx\) constant time but reset tires & sometimes strategy.

Interviews look for: ability to think in scenarios & probabilities, not perfect prediction.

8. Manufacturing & Materials

8.1 Composites
  • Carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP): high stiffness & strength, low weight.
  • Anisotropic behavior → ply orientation critical.
  • Sandwich panels: skins + honeycomb core (Nomex/aluminum) for stiffness & weight.
8.2 Practical Topics
  • Layup sequence, curing, quality control (voids, delamination).
  • Repair strategies: patching, re-laminating, NDT (ultrasound, tap test).
  • Machining tolerances and time-critical manufacturing (overnight parts between sessions).

9. Regulations, Cost Cap & Operations

9.1 FIA Sporting & Technical Constraints
  • Cost cap: Limits spending → optimization and prioritization.
  • Aero Testing Restrictions (ATR): Wind-tunnel/CFD hours depend on championship position.
  • Parc fermé: Limited changes after qualifying → setups must work in multiple conditions.
  • PU component limits: Penalties for extra engines, gearboxes, etc.
  • Plank wear, ride height & flexi-wings: legality under scrutineering.
9.2 Ops & Pit Stops
  • Sub-2s pit stops require repeatable, almost “industrial” execution.
  • Big emphasis on training, consistency, and safety (wheel nut engagement, lollipop/light system, etc.).

10. Behavioral & Mindset Must-Knows

10.1 What Teams Look For
  • Structured thinking: Can you break a messy problem into steps?
  • Calm under pressure: Especially for trackside & strategy roles.
  • Learning from failure: Post-event reviews, root-cause analysis.
  • Communication: Clear, concise, non-dramatic reporting of issues.
  • Passion with realism: Love for motorsport, but with a professional attitude.
10.2 Common Behavioral Questions
  • “Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete information.”
  • “Describe a failure and what you learned from it.”
  • “Give an example of working under extreme time pressure.”
  • “How do you handle conflict in a team?”
