Electric Circuit Calculations Quick Guide

Here are some key formulas for electric circuit calculations:

1. Ohm's Law

Ohm's law relates voltage, current, and resistance:

\[ V = I \cdot R \]

2. Power Formula

The power formula \( P = IV \) is used to calculate the power in an electrical circuit. Here's the mnemonic to remember it:

"If you have an IV, you have to pee" – This helps recall that power \( P \) is the product of current \( I \) and voltage \( V \).

Power can also be written as:

\[ P = I^2 \cdot R = \frac{V^2}{R} \]

3. Series Circuits

In a series circuit, the total resistance is the sum of individual resistances:

\[ R_\text{total} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \dots \]

For voltage division:

\[ V_n = V_\text{total} \cdot \frac{R_n}{R_\text{total}} \]

4. Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit, the total resistance is found by the reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances:

\[ \frac{1}{R_\text{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \dots \]

For current division:

\[ I_n = I_\text{total} \cdot \frac{R_\text{total}}{R_n} \]

5. Kirchhoff's Laws

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum of currents leaving it:

\[ \sum I_\text{in} = \sum I_\text{out} \]

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

The sum of voltages around any closed loop is zero:

\[ \sum V_\text{around a loop} = 0 \]

6. Capacitors and Inductors

Capacitors in Series and Parallel

For series capacitors:

\[ \frac{1}{C_\text{total}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \dots \]

For parallel capacitors:

\[ C_\text{total} = C_1 + C_2 + \dots \]

Inductors in Series and Parallel

For series inductors:

\[ L_\text{total} = L_1 + L_2 + \dots \]

For parallel inductors:

\[ \frac{1}{L_\text{total}} = \frac{1}{L_1} + \frac{1}{L_2} + \dots \]

7. AC Circuit Formulas

In AC circuits, impedance is given by:

\[ Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} \]

Where \( X_L = 2 \pi f L \) and \( X_C = \frac{1}{2 \pi f C} \).
Power in AC circuits is:

\[ P = V_\text{rms} \cdot I_\text{rms} \cdot \cos(\phi) \]
